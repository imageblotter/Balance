# Balance

A 3D tabletop balancing game for Android (libGDX + Bullet physics, written in Kotlin).

A white plate hangs from a single point above a table. Players take turns placing coloured cylinders on it
without tipping it over. The plate is a real pendulum: the further the load sits from the centre, the more
it tilts, and pieces start sliding.

## Rules

* 2–4 seats; each seat is a human or a computer player (skill: Easy / Normal / Hard).
* Everybody starts with 12 pieces: a **tall** and a **wide** cylinder in each of the six die colours.
* On your turn, roll the die. Place a piece **of the rolled colour**. If you have none left in that colour,
  you may place **any** piece.
* Every piece that falls off the plate – yours or anybody else's that you knocked off – goes into the
  inventory of the player who just placed.
* The first player with no pieces left after their turn wins.

## Controls

| Action | Touch | Desktop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pick the piece | tap its button | click |
| Move the piece | drag over the plate | drag |
| Turn the piece | twist with two fingers, or *Turn <* / *Turn >* | buttons |
| Lay a piece on its side | *Lay down* / *Stand up* | button |
| Drop | lift your finger after dragging | release the mouse |
| Look around | drag on the table, pinch to zoom, `<` `>` | drag, scroll wheel |

A ring on the plate marks where the held piece will land. A tap without dragging never drops a piece.

## Build and run

Requirements: JDK 17 or newer.

```
./gradlew :core:test            # rules, physics and full computer-vs-computer matches
./gradlew :desktop:run          # desktop build for quick iteration
./gradlew :android:assembleDebug  # APK in android/build/outputs/apk/debug/
```

`:android` is only part of the build when the Android SDK is found (`ANDROID_HOME`, `ANDROID_SDK_ROOT` or
`local.properties`). CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs the tests and builds the debug APK.

The desktop build can run headless for screenshots, see the comment at the top of
`desktop/src/main/kotlin/com/imageblotter/balance/desktop/DesktopLauncher.kt`.

## Layout

```
core/     all game code (platform independent)
  model/    GameState: turn rules, pieces, players – no physics or graphics
  physics/  BalanceWorld: the hanging plate and the pieces (Bullet)
  ai/       AiPlayer: picks piece and spot for computer players
  render/   Scene3D: models, camera, die animation, drop guide
  ui/       Hud (in-game overlay), UiKit (look and feel)
  screens/  MenuScreen, GameScreen (turn flow and touch input)
  audio/    Sfx: sound effects synthesised at start-up
desktop/  LWJGL3 launcher
android/  Android launcher
assets/   ui.fnt / ui.png (generated by tools/make_font.py from DejaVu Sans Bold)
```

## Tuning

Feel of the plate is set in `physics/Dimensions.kt`: a higher `PIVOT_HEIGHT` or a heavier `PLATE_MASS` makes the
plate more forgiving. `MatchSimulationTest` plays complete games between computer players against the real
physics and prints how often pieces fall at each skill level, which is the quickest way to check a change.
